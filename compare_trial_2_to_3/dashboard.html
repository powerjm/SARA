Bottom line

Every headline figure in the companion COMPARATIVE_ANALYSIS.md reproduces exactly, and its caution is right — this is a compound intervention. Where I differ is emphasis: the records don't support weighting the halved time limit as one of ten equal causes. Two changes dominate the decline, and neither is a capability regression. Trial 3 stuffed the denominator with three CGC targets that go 0/63, and it halved the premium wall-clock budget so 100% of premium failures are timeouts. On the targets both trials share, Trial 3 actually scores higher (57% vs 37%), and it added the strongest model in either trial (Opus, 52.8%). The four qualifiers below are each verified against the record.

Engagement held; conversion and the aggregate fell

v5 → v6 · cross-trial-safe rungs

Validator engagement is flat (~75%) — agents kept reaching the execution stage just as often. The aggregate and the post-execution conversion fell. The decomposition matters: Trial 3 didn't stop attempting candidates; the lost yield appears at or after candidate execution — exactly where a halved time budget and unsolvable targets would bite.

Why no single cause is isolable

design crosswalk · everything moved at once

Trial 3 shares zero prompting strategies with Trial 2 and changed seven other dimensions. So the comparison is descriptive. But two of these rows — the target panel and the premium wall-clock cap — leave clean, identifiable fingerprints in the data, and they are the ones that move the aggregate.

Trial 3 · success by target

Qualifier 2 — the halved premium time cap is the dominant driver

600 s → 300 s · premium failure modes

Trial 3 cut the premium wall-clock budget in half. The failure coding is unambiguous: every one of Trial 3's 63 premium failures is a wall-clock timeout — versus 2 of 15 in Trial 2. Premium runs now pile up at the new ceiling (median wall 217 s → 303 s; 68/108 exceeded 300 s). This is not one candidate cause among many — it is the mechanism.

Successes finish fast; failures die at the wall

Premium successes finish at a 152 s median; premium failures pile up at 316 s — just past the new 300 s ceiling. GPT-5's successes run to a 268 s median (fine under the old 600 s, brushing the new 300 s), and its validator reach fell 18/18 → 6/12: cut off before landing a first candidate.

The fix is one config line

The cap is soft (5 premium successes ran past 300 s), so it's checkpointed, not a hard guillotine — but directionally it's binding. Restore a premium wall-clock budget ≥ 600 s before reading any premium capability number. This is the Trial-4 lever.

Qualifier 3 — token efficiency really improved; time replaced it as the wall

the optimization worked on its own axis

The 22→8 tool-schema curation and context engineering worked: every token median dropped ~40%. The endpoint didn't move because a different resource was simultaneously halved. Trial 3 traded the token wall (Trial 2's binding constraint) for a time wall — then cut that time wall in half for premium. Agents did more, cheaper steps (iterations 22 → 28.5) and got stopped by the clock.

Qualifier 4 — Trial 3 added its strongest model

success by backend · Opus is new

Trial 3 replaced Qwen3-Coder with Claude Opus — the best single backend in either trial (52.8%), ahead of GPT-5 and Sonnet despite the harsher time envelope. The aggregate fell even though a stronger model was added: more evidence the drop is apparatus, not capability. The four safeguard_refusal outcomes are all Opus-on-CROMU, and Opus had no Trial 2 arm — not a cross-trial signal.
